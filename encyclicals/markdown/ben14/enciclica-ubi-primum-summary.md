## Summary

*Ubi Primum* (Latin: "When First") is the inaugural encyclical of Pope Benedict XIV, issued on December 3, 1740, shortly after his election to the papacy. The document is a pastoral exhortation addressed to the Catholic episcopate, outlining the pope's vision for Church governance and the duties of bishops. It focuses heavily on reforming the clergy — their selection, formation, discipline, and ongoing pastoral commitment — and insists that bishops personally reside in their dioceses, visit their flocks, and lead by example.

## Historical Context

Benedict XIV (born Prospero Lorenzo Lambertini) was elected pope in August 1740 after a contentious six-month conclave. He inherited a Church still shaped by the reforms of the Council of Trent (1545–1563), which had mandated seminaries, episcopal residence, and clerical discipline — but whose decrees were unevenly implemented across Europe. The 18th century also saw the rise of Enlightenment rationalism, regalist movements (where monarchs asserted control over local Church affairs), and Jansenist rigorism in France and the Low Countries. *Ubi Primum* was Benedict's opening statement of intent: a return to Trent's core vision, a call to clerical integrity, and an assertion of the bishop's personal responsibility for his diocese at a time when absenteeism and laxity remained widespread problems.

## Key Themes

- **Clerical selection and quality over quantity:** Bishops must admit only well-formed, virtuous candidates to holy orders, resisting pressure from patrons or local interests; it is better to have few worthy priests than many unsuitable ones.
- **Seminary reform and education:** Where seminaries do not exist, they must be founded; existing ones should be expanded and personally supervised by the bishop, who should inspect students, faculty, and academic progress.
- **Episcopal residence and personal vigilance:** Bishops must reside in their own dioceses — absence requires a genuine, proportionate cause — because a shepherd who is absent cannot defend his flock, and vicars cannot replace the bishop's own pastoral presence.
- **Preaching and catechesis:** Parish priests must regularly preach sound doctrine and, critically, teach the catechism to children; the encyclical calls this duty "impossible to fully comprehend" in the harm caused by its neglect.
- **Diocesan visitation:** Bishops must personally and repeatedly visit all parts of their diocese, not merely issue decrees, but verify that reforms are actually implemented.
- **Bishop as living model:** The bishop is to be an exemplar of virtue in liturgy, charity, and daily conduct, so that clergy and faithful may imitate him as he imitates Christ.

## Significance

*Ubi Primum* established Benedict XIV's reputation as a reforming, pastoral pope and set the tone for a pontificate widely regarded as one of the most learned and capable of the 18th century. While not a doctrinal or dogmatic document, it is a significant milestone in the reception of Tridentine reform, reasserting the Council of Trent's core disciplines (seminaries, residence, visitation) at a time when they were flagging. The encyclical's emphases — particularly on episcopal residence and personal accountability — would echo in later magisterial documents, including Vatican II's *Christus Dominus*. Benedict XIV's practical, clear-sighted pastoral approach in *Ubi Primum* earned him respect even from Enlightenment critics and helped define the model of the resident bishop for centuries to come.
